## 1. Accepted kinematic model of thumb-tip motion

**The consensus biomechanical model treats the trapeziometacarpal (CMC/TMC) joint as 2 rotational DOF about two FIXED, NON-ORTHOGONAL, NON-INTERSECTING (skew) axes**, established by Hollister, Buford, Myers, Giurintano & Novick, *"The axes of rotation of the thumb carpometacarpal joint"*, J. Orthop. Res. 10(3):454–460, 1992 ([paper via Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22The+axes+of+rotation+of+the+thumb+carpometacarpal+joint%22+Hollister)). Their key findings:
- The **flexion–extension (FE) axis is fixed in the trapezium**; the **abduction–adduction (AA) axis is fixed in the first metacarpal**; neither axis is perpendicular to the bones or to each other ([PubMed summary](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Hollister+axes+rotation+thumb+carpometacarpal)).
- Follow-up modeling literature quantifies the skew as an inter-axis angle of ≈124° (SD ≈19°) with an inter-axis offset of ≈9.2 mm (numbers from later studies building on Hollister's method, surfaced in an NIH-indexed source during search — treat as representative, verify against the specific paper before publishing). Because the axes are fixed and skewed, the articular surfaces behave like **"skewed tori"** rather than an orthogonal saddle.
- The joint additionally exhibits coupled **pronation–supination (axial rotation)**: Cooney, Lucca, Chao & Linscheid, *"The kinesiology of the thumb trapeziometacarpal joint"*, JBJS 63-A(9):1371–1381, 1981 ([Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Cooney+kinesiology+thumb+trapeziometacarpal+joint+1981)) measured average TMC ROM of **53° flexion–extension, 42° abduction–adduction, 17° axial rotation** via roentgenographic markers. The 17° axial rotation is largely kinematically coupled to FE/AA (it is what lets the pad "oppose"), which matters for key-top orientation.
- Robotics confirms the skew-axis requirement: the CMU **ACT (Anatomically Correct Testbed) hand thumb** (Chang & Matsuoka, *"A Kinematic Thumb Model for the ACT Hand"*, ICRA 2006, [CMU RI page](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/a-kinematic-thumb-model-for-the-act-hand/)) is a **5-DOF serial chain (2 CMC + 2 MCP + 1 IP) with deliberately non-orthogonal, non-intersecting axes**, and the same group showed that simplified orthogonal/intersecting-axis thumb models **mispredict the tip workspace and endpoint forces** — the direct argument for not modeling the thumb as a gimbal.

**What curve does the TIP trace with the palm planted?**
- Pure FE at the CMC (distal joints held) is rotation about ONE fixed axis, so the tip traces a **circular arc lying in a plane perpendicular to the FE axis**, with radius = perpendicular distance from tip to that axis. This is why circle/sphere fitting to motion-capture thumb-tip trajectories is a standard technique for locating the functional pivot and radius of curvature (e.g., sphere/circle-fitting of thumb-tip workspaces in reachable-workspace studies, [UCSF/eScholarship workspace modeling](https://escholarship.org/), [markerless mocap CMC studies on PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/)).
- **Plane orientation relative to the palm:** classical hand anatomy (Kapandji; standard kinesiology references, e.g., [OUHSC hand kinesiology notes](https://www.ouhsc.edu/bserdac/dthompso/web/namics/thumb.htm)) defines thumb **flexion–extension as occurring in a plane roughly PARALLEL to the palm** (thumb sweeps across the palm) and **palmar abduction–adduction in a plane roughly PERPENDICULAR to the palm**. So to first order the FE axis ≈ palm normal — but Hollister's result says it is oblique, tilted away from the exact palm normal and not orthogonal to the AA axis, so the real FE arc plane is **tilted ~10–25° from palm-parallel and the arc plane also rides ~1–2 cm above the desk when the palm is planted** (tilt magnitude is my synthesis of "non-orthogonal, non-perpendicular-to-bones"; the papers give axis orientations relative to bone landmarks, not the palm plane — a hand scan should FIT this tilt rather than assume it).
- Full 2-DOF **circumduction traces a cone-like closed loop**, i.e., the tip moves on an approximately spherical/toroidal shell around the CMC region ([PMC review of CMC kinematics](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/), thumb-workspace modeling literature above). With skew offset axes the exact shell is a **torus patch** (offset d≈9 mm between axes), which for a 3-key cluster is indistinguishable from a sphere of radius R±d.
- **Quantitative tip-sweep radius from HCI research:** for one-handed phone grips, the comfortable thumb sweep is an **arc of ≈63–76 mm (2.5–3 in) radius about the thumb base**, with extreme reach measured up to **97.5 mm** (Bergström-Lehtovirta & Oulasvirta, *"Modeling the functional area of the thumb on mobile touchscreen surfaces"*, CHI 2014, [PDF via Aalto](http://users.comnet.aalto.fi/oulasvir/pubs/)); Steven Hoober's thumb-zone work popularized the arc-shaped "natural zone" ([uxmatters series](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2017/03/design-for-fingers-touch-and-people-part-1.php)). These radii are measured mid-flexion (bent MCP/IP), which matches the typing posture better than full-extension anthropometric thumb length (~100–120 mm from CMC).

## 2. How ergonomic keyboard designers actually place thumb clusters

**Cosmos (ryanis.cool/cosmos)** — the most explicit generator:
- The webcam **hand scan measures only phalanx bone lengths** and drives keywell row/column stagger; the docs state the fitting algorithm is "primitive" and the **thumb cluster is NOT auto-placed from scan data** ([Cosmos hand-scans docs](https://ryanis.cool/cosmos/docs/hand-scans/)).
- Thumb presets: **Manuform, Carbonfet, Orbyl, Curved**; the **"Curved" preset with 3 or 5 keys is the recommended comfort option**, and users are told to experiment with **negative curvature** ([Cosmos docs / tips](https://ryanis.cool/cosmos/docs/)). The whole cluster is moved/rotated as a rigid unit (press "P"), i.e., placement is manual 6-DOF.
- **Expert mode is the quantitative core**: thumb keys are placed with `placeOnSphere({curvature, spacing, angle, row})`, which arranges keys **along concentric circles**: `angle` = position along the circle, `row` = number of `spacing` (mm) intervals from the sphere center, `curvature` = per-step tilt so key normals stay tangent to the sphere ([Cosmos expert-mode docs](https://ryanis.cool/cosmos/docs/expert/), source: [github.com/rianadon/Cosmos-Keyboards](https://github.com/rianadon/Cosmos-Keyboards)). Main keywell uses `placeOnMatrix` (two cylinder curvatures). This is exactly a spherical-cap parameterization of the thumb fan.

**Dactyl / Dactyl-Manuform:**
- Original Dactyl (Matt Adereth, [github.com/adereth/dactyl-keyboard](https://github.com/adereth/dactyl-keyboard), talk *clojure.core/typing* [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uk3A41U0iO4)) places the thumb keys on a concave well generated in Clojure like the torus-section finger wells.
- Dactyl-Manuform (Tom Short, [github.com/tshort/dactyl-keyboard](https://github.com/tshort/dactyl-keyboard)) swaps in the ManuForm thumb cluster: **each thumb key gets an individual Euler rotation `[rx ry rz]` plus translation `[tx ty tz]` from a `thumborigin` anchor near the bottom inner index-column key** (with a small global `thumb-offsets` vector); typical per-key rotations in the source are in the **±10–25° range per axis** and offsets of order 10–60 mm. There is **no underlying curve model** — the six keys are hand-tuned poses, which is exactly the pain point your model would fix ([community explanation of the rotation/translation vectors, r/ErgoMechKeyboards threads](https://www.reddit.com/r/ErgoMechKeyboards/)).

**Bastard Keyboards (Skeletyl / Charybdis, [bastardkb.com](https://bastardkb.com/)):** deliberately ship **3-key thumb clusters** (Skeletyl, Charybdis Nano) on the rationale that only keys inside the thumb's no-stretch zone should exist, with layers (Miryoku) replacing key count; on Charybdis the cluster is co-placed with the trackball so the thumb transitions between ball and keys without hand movement ([bastardkb.com product/docs pages](https://docs.bastardkb.com/)).

**Keyboardio Model 01/100 ([keyboard.io](https://shop.keyboard.io/)):** 4 thumb keys per hand arranged **along an arc under the palm** ("thumb arc") sized so adjacent modifiers can be chorded by one thumb; rationale is offloading pinkies and echoing natural thumb sweep, but no published radius — the arc was tuned through physical prototyping (Jesse Vincent's design write-ups/interviews).

**Community consensus numbers** (r/ErgoMechKeyboards, kbd.news):
- Only **1–2 thumb keys are comfortable for high-frequency use; 2–3 max are reachable without stretch**; extra keys get demoted to rare functions ([community consensus threads](https://www.reddit.com/r/ErgoMechKeyboards/), [bastardkb rationale](https://bastardkb.com/)).
- kbd.news distinguishes two schools: **"thumb blocks"** (Kinesis/ErgoDox plates) vs **"thumb curves"** (Keyboardio, Kyria, Iris) that align few keys **along the thumb's arc**; sloped/dished clusters beat flat ones; most-used key should sit ≤0.5u from the thumb's resting spot; convex keycaps preferred because the thumb strikes obliquely ([kbd.news, tags "thumb cluster"/"ergonomics"](https://kbd.news/)).
- In **Ergogen** ([docs.ergogen.xyz](https://docs.ergogen.xyz/)), thumb fans are built with per-key `rotate` around a shared origin plus `splay` — i.e., a discrete circular arc in 2D, tented afterwards.
- Practical fit check: print layouts 1:1 with [compare.splitkb.com](https://compare.splitkb.com/) and see where the thumb lands.

## 3. Projects with EXPLICIT curve parameterizations
1. **Cosmos `placeOnSphere`** — spherical cap: key i at azimuth `angle_i` on circle `row_i · spacing` from center, normal tilted by `curvature · row` to stay sphere-tangent ([expert docs](https://ryanis.cool/cosmos/docs/expert/)). The only mainstream generator with a closed-form thumb curve.
2. **Original Dactyl** — thumb keys on the same torus/cylinder-section machinery as the keywell (row radius + column radius parameters in `dactyl.clj`, [source](https://github.com/adereth/dactyl-keyboard)).
3. **Ergogen splay/rotate zones** — 2D circular fan about a declared origin ([docs](https://docs.ergogen.xyz/)).
4. **Lalboard (JesusFreke, [github.com/JesusFreke/lalboard](https://github.com/JesusFreke/lalboard)) and its commercial successor Svalboard ([svalboard.com](https://svalboard.com/))** — per-finger/thumb clusters with **5–6-DOF adjustable mounts fitted empirically** (Svalboard publishes a 5-step fit procedure: palmrest 3-axis, then per-cluster X/Y/Z/roll/yaw); users have clay-molded or 3D-scanned hands to freeze positions. **No analytic curve** — it's measured-pose capture, the empirical endpoint your model should approximate analytically.
5. Dactyl-Manuform and most forks: **no curve**, hand-tuned per-key poses (see §2).

## 4. Proposed candidate mathematical models for a 3-key cluster

Common frame: palm plane Π with origin at planted pisiform/heel, x = ulnar→radial, y = distal, z = palm normal. Let P = CMC pivot (palpable at base of thenar eminence, ~ scaphoid tubercle).

**Model A — Single circular FE arc about an oblique CMC axis (recommended default).**
K(θ) = P + R·(cosθ·u + sinθ·v), where {u,v,n} orthonormal, n = FE-axis direction = Rz(β)·Rx(α)·ẑ (α,β = tilt of the arc plane away from palm-parallel; expect α,β ≈ 10–25° per Hollister's non-orthogonality, fitted not assumed). Keys at θ_i = θ0 + i·Δθ, i∈{0,1,2}, with chord spacing s: Δθ = 2·asin(s/2R). With s = 19.05 mm (1u) and R = 75 mm (mid comfortable sweep 63–76 mm, CHI 2014): **Δθ ≈ 14.6°, total sweep ≈ 29° — comfortably inside Cooney's 53° FE ROM**. Key normal: start from in-plane radial direction −(cosθ·u+sinθ·v), rotate about the local tangent by opposition/pronation angle ψ ≈ 30–60° so the cap faces the thumb pad (community "sloped cluster" + Cooney's 17° axial coupling ⇒ optionally ψ_i = ψ0 + (17°/53°)·(θ_i−θ0)). Pros: minimal parameters (P, R, n, θ0, ψ0), adjacent keys reached by pure 1-DOF flexion (cheapest motion), directly fittable by circle-fit to 5+ scanned tip points during a planted-palm sweep. Cons: assumes constant R (true only if MCP/IP posture held); ignores AA.
Scan needs: thumb-tip positions at 5+ poses of a comfortable planted-palm sweep, +1 nail-plane orientation.

**Model B — Spherical cap with twist (Cosmos-compatible generalization).**
K(θ,φ) = P + R·(sinφ cosθ, sinφ sinθ, cosφ) in the CMC frame; 3 keys either along one parallel (φ const → reduces to Model A) or 2+1 across parallels for an inner "tucked" key; normals radial; twist ψ(θ) as above. This is exactly `placeOnSphere(curvature = Δφ per row, spacing = R·Δφ, angle = θ)` ([Cosmos expert docs](https://ryanis.cool/cosmos/docs/expert/)), so output is directly usable in Cosmos. Pros: captures both DOF, supports later 4th/5th key. Cons: sphere center ≠ true pivot pair (skew axes), one extra fitted angle.
Scan needs: tip positions over a small 2-DOF patch (sweep + one abducted/adducted repeat).

**Model C — Hollister-faithful two-skew-axis torus.**
K(θFE,θAA) = Rot(A1,θFE)·[ Rot(A2,θAA)·(T0 − P2) + P2 − P1 ] + P1 with axis lines (P1,a1) fixed in trapezium/palm frame and (P2,a2) riding on the metacarpal, inter-axis angle ≈124°, offset ≈9.2 mm (§1 caveats), T0 = rest tip. 3 keys = samples at (θFE = {−Δ,0,+Δ}, θAA = 0). Pros: anatomically exact; predicts the tilted, slightly helical key line (twist emerges naturally instead of being bolted on). Cons: axes are NOT recoverable from a static hand scan — Hollister needed an axis-finder jig; you'd need video/mocap of an isolated sweep, and for 3 keys it collapses to Model A within measurement noise (offset 9 mm ≪ R 75 mm ⇒ tip-position difference <1.5 mm over a 30° sweep).

**Recommendation:** fit Model A from a 5-point planted-palm sweep (circle fit gives P⊥, R, n in one shot); expose Model B's φ as the upgrade path; treat Model C as validation-only. Sanity anchors: R ∈ [60, 95] mm, inter-key pitch 14–17°, total 3-key sweep ≤ 35° (≈⅔ of the 53° FE ROM), cluster plate pitched 30–60° from palm plane toward the pad, most-used key at the REST pose θ0 (not mid-arc) per community guidance.
